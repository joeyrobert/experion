A chess engine written in Crystal, with a neural network evaluation.

Experion is a UCI engine with an NNUE evaluation trained from scratch on 142 million positions from CCRL games. It beats Fruit 2.1 and Crafty 25.2 in local matches, ships as a single self-contained executable, and runs in your browser.

OpponentResultScoreElo
Fruit 2.169–17–1476%+200 ± 74
Crafty 25.258–32–1063%+92 ± 68

Experion ran on 4 threads; both opponents ran on one, at their default settings, and Crafty had its own clock through an XBoard bridge. Intervals are 95%. These are local head-to-head numbers, not rating-list ratings. The browser version runs on one thread, so it plays weaker than these figures.

How it works

Everything is written in Crystal, including the network inference. The training pipeline is Python and PyTorch.

NNUE evaluation

768 piece-square inputs across 8 mirrored king buckets feed 256 SCReLU units per side, with output buckets by piece count and a learned material lane. Accumulators update incrementally, and a per-bucket cache keeps king moves cheap.

Search

Iterative-deepening PVS with a transposition table, null-move pruning, late-move reductions, reverse futility, SEE-pruned quiescence, history heuristics and lazy SMP. Features that measured as losses were removed.

Trained from CCRL games

The extractor detects each game's score convention, deduplicates positions and keeps quiet ones. One consumer GPU trains the net in about half an hour. The full pipeline is in the repository.

Runs anywhere

Static Linux binary, macOS for Apple silicon and Intel, Windows, and a WebAssembly build for this site. The network is embedded, so there is nothing else to install.

Engine arena

The play page lets each side be a human or an engine, so you can play any of them or watch two play each other. Everything runs in your browser.

Your own engines

A small local bridge lets the page drive native engines on your computer, such as Crafty or the multi-threaded desktop Experion. Credits and sources.
